About

52 Years in Tech. Still Learning.

I touched my first computer in 1974. I was 12 years old, connecting via teletype to the University of Pennsylvania's ENIAC system. The idea that I could talk to a machine through a phone line? It blew my mind.


In 1983, I built DECADE 90 — a BBS running WW3 software. Two phone lines, a 2-meter packet radio server for email, and a movie database that people actually used. We were a hub connecting Philadelphia to Moorestown, NJ. This was years before the internet existed.


After the Army (8.5 years as an Air Defense Crew member), I kept building. Three ISPs. A large MSP for 9.5 years. A train carrier. Then I landed at my current job — a large worldwide insurance company — as a contractor for 2 years before they brought me on full-time.


Here's why I teach AI: My boss trained 5,000 insurance workers on MS365 Copilot. I saw how confused people get. Everyone's scared AI will replace them. But I've watched technology transform industries my whole life — and the people who adapt always win.


I'm not here to teach you to code. I'm here to help everyday workers use AI to automate the boring stuff and focus on work that actually matters.
